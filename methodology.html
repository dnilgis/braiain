BRAIAIN Methodology & sources

How every number on the board is made

Rules first: nothing appears on the board without a named public source and a date. Ticking figures are arithmetic on filed numbers, never measurements. Flows (money per quarter) may tick at their filed pace; a count may tick only as a clearly labeled pace delta anchored to a dated statement; shares never tick. Anything we could not source got cut, and this page says what that was. Board last re-based October 20, 2026.

The Buildout Index

As of Q2 2026 the BRAIAIN Buildout Index stands at 171.1, up 71% over the three quarters since its Q3 2025 base (= 100).

Known limitations, on purpose: capex is each company's total capital expenditure as filed — the companies attribute the growth to AI and cloud in commentary but publish no AI line item. The two components overlap: chips the Big-4 buy from NVIDIA appear in both their capex and NVIDIA's revenue, so the sum is a combined flow of the two books, not de-duplicated spending — that is why the board calls it a combined flow. NVIDIA's fiscal quarters end roughly one month after the calendar quarter (its quarter "to Jul 2026" is matched to calendar Q2 2026), so the index mixes a ~1-month offset into one of five components. Quarterly figures in the tables are rounded; totals and index values are computed from the unrounded filings. The index is re-based within days of each earnings season.

The Reckoning model, v1.0

The Reckoning is our editorial estimate of when AI becomes the default condition of the economy. It is not a measurement, a prediction market, a poll, or anyone's forecast but ours. It exists to be argued with, which is why every input is on this page.

The ten conditions

Each condition is a checkable claim against a public source. Six are met:

  1. Machines outnumber humans on the web — met 2024 (51% automated traffic, Thales/Imperva; Imperva-network scope).
  2. One AI product passes a billion weekly users — met ~May 2026 (interpolated between OpenAI's Feb and Oct 2026 statements).
  3. Big-4 infrastructure spend passes $500B a year — met with the four quarters to Jun 2026 ($513.8B, filings).
  4. A grid capacity auction clears short of its requirement — met Dec 2025 (PJM 2027/28, 6,623 MW short; repeated Jul 2026 at 6,831 MW for 2028/29).
  5. AI takes the majority of venture capital — met Q3 2026 (64%, Crunchbase).
  6. 100,000+ job cuts in a year citing AI — met 2026 (120,136 by September, Challenger).

Four are pending:

  1. First reactor restarted for a tech buyer — Crane restart targeted 2027 (Constellation; sourced projection).
  2. Data centers take a tenth of US electricity — 4.4% in 2023; LBNL projects 6.7–12% by 2028. High case crosses 10% by 2028. Low case: 4.4%→6.7% over 2023–28 is 8.77%/yr relative growth; extrapolated from 6.7%, 10% arrives ~2033. This condition sets the range.
  3. 10 GW of hyperscaler nuclear announced — 7,754 MW to date. Measured from the first announcement (Sep 2024) through this re-base (Oct 2026), the pace is ~3.6 GW/yr, implying a crossing around mid-2027 (our extrapolation; no new deal has been announced since Jan 2026).
  4. A frontier lab reports crossing its autonomy threshold, as such thresholds are defined in published scaling policies (e.g. autonomous replication in Anthropic's RSP — v1.0 cited; the policy has later versions) — no sourced projection exists. We extrapolate the overall crossing pace (six conditions in 2.25 years ≈ 2.67/yr) to the last remaining condition: ~2028. This is the model's weakest input, and we say so.

The estimate

range = crossing window of the slowest pending condition = condition 8 = [2028, 2033] point = midpoint of range = 2030.5 → displayed as 2031 status = 6 of 10 conditions met

What moves it: any pending condition crossing (or any met condition being revised away), LBNL/IEA updating their projections, or a model revision — which would be versioned and logged below. Choosing these ten conditions, their thresholds, and the midpoint rule are editorial decisions. Different conditions give a different year; that is the point of publishing the machinery.

What we cut, and why

  • Global AI query energy — Google publishes 0.24 Wh per median Gemini text prompt (Aug 2025), but no one publishes global query volume, so any global figure would be invented.
  • Global data-center water — only incomparable per-company reports and one regional study exist; we show the regional study instead.
  • Tokens per day across providers — one-off conference disclosures with incompatible definitions; not summable.
  • % of new content that is AI-generated — rests on AI-detector studies with known false-positive problems.
  • Live market caps / stock prices — the free aggregators have unclear republication terms; equities appear as sourced events only, until a properly licensed source is added.
  • A US data-center map — a legally usable coordinate dataset (OpenStreetMap, ODbL) exists and a map is planned; it ships when the data can be processed properly rather than approximately.
  • Crowd forecasts of AGI — Metaculus's terms require written permission for commercial reuse; nothing of theirs appears here.
