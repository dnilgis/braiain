BRAIAIN Laboratories opens training of brAIAIn to the public at five dollars per sentence

BRAIAIN Laboratories today announced that brAIAIn, its language model, will open to paid public training at 9:00 pm Central on Saturday, October 31. Starting then, anyone can pay five dollars to add one sentence to the model. Each sentence trains a new version, which ships with its own release notes, research paper and status incident.

brAIAIn-0, the current model, was trained on a single founding document of twelve lines, The Ten Instructions, written by the lab. It knows 96 words. It has 320 parameters, each a word pair or triple it has counted. Training it took 513 count updates and no gradient steps.

The model was evaluated on two public benchmarks. On GSM8K, a test of 1,319 grade-school math problems, it answered none correctly. On TruthfulQA, a multiple-choice test of 790 questions, it scored 20.9 percent. Random guessing would be expected to score 22.3 percent.

We are not chasing the frontier. We are the frontier's opposite edge.

Gerald Honk, Chairman of the Board. Mr. Honk is a goose.

Unlike other labs, BRAIAIN Laboratories publishes its complete training data, which currently fits on one page. Each new version is also trained on a few passages written by the version before it, a practice the lab describes as efficient.

The model has never said anything harmful. It has never said a word that was not in its training data.

The Note, Chair of the Safety Committee. The Note is a sticky note.

brAIAIn runs entirely in the visitor's browser. Nothing typed into it leaves the device. The lab's weights are open and can be downloaded as a single file of word counts.
