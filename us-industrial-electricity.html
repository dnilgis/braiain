US industrial electricity price trend

US industrial electricity averaged 8.62 cents/kWh in 2025 (preliminary), up 29% from 6.67 cents in 2020 — after holding between 6.7 and 7.2 cents for the entire decade before. The most recent monthly figure, July 2026, is 9.77 cents, up 4.7% year over year. Figures are nominal.

Method & caveats

EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.3 (2016–2025) and Electric Power Annual Table 2.4 (2014–2015); the tables agree in overlapping years. The 2025 annual figure is preliminary. Prices are nominal and national — this series shows the trend; it does not attribute the rise to any single cause, including AI data centers. For the AI-load context, see the PJM capacity-price series (a separate market measuring a different thing).

Questions

Are data centers raising US electricity prices?
This series alone cannot say — it is nominal, national, and driven by fuel costs, inflation and demand together. What it shows: a decade of flat prices through 2021, then +29% by 2025. The capacity-market signal (PJM) is on its own page.
How much power do data centers use?
LBNL/DOE estimated US data centers at 176 TWh — about 4.4% of US electricity — in 2023, projected to 6.7–12% by 2028. Globally the IEA estimates 415 TWh (2024), projected to ~945 TWh by 2030.
