How much are Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spending on capex?

In the four quarters ending June 30, 2026, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta reported $513.8 billion of combined capital expenditure — Amazon $173.0B, Alphabet $132.4B, Microsoft $115.9B, Meta $92.4B. The companies describe the growth as AI- and cloud-driven in commentary, but none reports an AI line item, so these are total-capex figures as filed.

Method & caveats

Each company's own headline capex definition is used as filed: Microsoft and Alphabet report cash purchases of property and equipment; Amazon reports gross purchases (not netted for proceeds); Meta includes finance-lease principal, matching the figure its releases state. Quarters not reported directly are derived by subtracting year-to-date figures within or across the company's own filings; every derivation is recorded in the data file. The index column adds NVIDIA's data-center segment revenue, whose fiscal quarters end about a month after calendar quarters; chips the big 4 buy from NVIDIA appear in both components, so the combined flow is a sum of reported lines with the overlap not removed — it is labeled accordingly wherever it appears.

Questions

Do these companies report AI capex separately?
No. All four report total capital expenditure and attribute its growth to AI and cloud infrastructure in commentary. Any 'AI capex' figure you see elsewhere is an estimate.
How fast is this spending growing?
The combined quarterly flow (big-4 capex plus NVIDIA data-center revenue) went from $39.4B in Q1 2022 to $255.0B in Q2 2026 — about 55% a year compounded, with a dip in early 2023.
What was guided for next year?
Meta guided 2026 capex of $130–145B in its Q2 2026 release. Alphabet and Amazon capex guidance ($195–205B and ~$220B) appeared in earnings-call coverage rather than the releases, so those two are weaker-sourced.
